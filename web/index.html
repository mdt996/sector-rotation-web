Rotation rule

Every Friday close, rank the 11 sectors by their 12-month return, leaving out the latest month (moves over the last few weeks tend to reverse). The top — are that Friday's picks, in equal parts. Move a quarter of the money you rotate each Friday, so the target split is the average of the last four Fridays and no single week decides it. Volatility and the 200-day trend are shown for context, not used: in the 15-year test below, keeping only sectors above their 200-day average gave a smaller worst drop but a lower return.

Your holdings

Each holding read through the US fund or stock it follows (a European semiconductor ETF through SMH, for example), with the rotation rule's yardstick: its 12-month return without the latest month. As strong as the weakest of the rule's picks: Hold, strong. Weaker: Hold, lagging, with the rule's strongest pick as the alternative. Down over the year: Reduce. Long-term holds are never told to sell, only when adding looks timely. Returns are converted to euros. With euro amounts (typed in the app's Settings), each holding shows its share of the total, and a suggested move shows the euros to shift each week, a quarter at a time. How the numbers and signals are worked out
Last month
The last — calendar days, close to close. 1W, 3M and 6M work the same way.
vs SPY
How much the ETF beat or trailed SPY over the last month.
Pace shift
Last week's return minus the average weekly return of the weeks before it in the month. Negative means the move is slowing, the first sign of a leader rolling over.
Phase
From a relative rotation graph. Relative strength is the ETF's price divided by SPY's, smoothed over — sessions. RS-Ratio compares it with its own average over — sessions (above 100: beating the market). RS-Momentum is the change in RS-Ratio over — sessions (above 100: getting stronger). Leading = both above, Weakening = ratio above but momentum below, Lagging = both below, Improving = ratio below but momentum above.
Fading
Weakening; or Leading with RS-Momentum at least — points below its recent peak; or Lagging after leading or weakening a couple of weeks ago.
Support and resistance
Swing highs and lows (the extreme of — sessions either side) over the last — sessions, grouped when they sit within a fraction of the average daily range. The nearest below is support, the nearest above resistance; with none, the one-year low or high.
Exit and entry triggers
Exit is the latest swing low and entry the latest swing high within — sessions. A daily close below the exit says the up-move has broken; a close above the entry confirms a new one.
Actions
Hold: leading and healthy. Hold, overbought: RSI at — or more. Hold, tighten stop: momentum fading or price under the —-day average. Trim: weakening but still above that average. Rotate out: weakening and below it, or the exit fired. Accumulate: improving and above the average, or the entry fired. Watch and Avoid otherwise.
Weekly view
The same RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum on weekly closes: smoothed over — weeks, compared with its —-week average, momentum over — weeks, tails of — weeks. The current week counts up to the last close. When the daily and weekly phases agree, the move is broader.
Market regime
From SPY against its 50- and 200-day averages, how many of the 11 sectors close above their 50-day average (at least — is broad, — or fewer is narrow), and whether defensive sectors (staples, utilities, health care) beat cyclical ones (discretionary, financials, industrials, materials, technology) by 3 points or more over the month, or discretionary is falling behind staples. Volatility is the annualized spread of SPY's last — daily moves, ranked against the last year.
Rotation rule
Each sector's 12-month return leaving out the latest month: the close — sessions ago against the close — sessions ago, taken on the same SPY sessions for every fund. Every Friday close the top — are the picks; the target split averages the last — Fridays' picks, a quarter each. Volatility is the yearly-scaled spread of daily returns over the last — sessions; return ÷ volatility divides the two.
15-year study
The rule replayed on weekly eToro closes with only what was known at each Friday close, trading at the next week's open; the monthly switch is run as four sleeves, each reset every four weeks one week apart. Prices only: dividends would add roughly one to three points a year to every line, a bit more for utilities, energy, staples and real estate. The best split in hindsight is the long-only mix with the highest return per unit of volatility over the period, found after the fact. Sector rankings have shifted a lot from period to period, which is why copying the past best split is shown as its own test.
Your holdings
Each holding is mapped to the US fund or stock it follows. Its 12-month return without the latest month is compared with the weakest of the rule's current top sectors: as strong or stronger is Hold, strong; weaker is Hold, lagging; below zero is Reduce. Rotate into names the strongest of the rule's picks you don't already hold. Returns in euros add the euro's move against the dollar over the same days.
Data
Daily candles for the US-listed ETFs from eToro (your API keys) or Yahoo Finance, refreshed when you open the app and about every hour in the background. The background check uses completed daily closes only and notifies you when something changes. Volumes and prices are the data source's.
Left out
Dividends, fees, currency (the UCITS versions trade in euros or dollars on European exchanges) and taxes. This is a timing aid, not advice.
